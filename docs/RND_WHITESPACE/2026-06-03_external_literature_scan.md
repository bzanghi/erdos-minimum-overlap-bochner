## Headline takeaways

1. **The "proof-grade dual certificate" the project wants is a published, named technique — and there are TWO independent routes, one of which bypasses the SDPA-GMP serializer entirely.**
   - Route A (snap-the-dual, no serializer): **Davis & Papp, "Dual certificates and efficient rational SOS decompositions over compact sets"** (arXiv:2105.11369, SIAM J. Opt. 2022) + **"Rational Dual Certificates for WSOS Polynomials with Boundable Bit Size"** (arXiv:2305.19039, 2023). Verbatim: *"rational WSOS certificates can be constructed from numerically computed dual certificates at little additional cost, without any rounding or projection steps,"* working on top of standard interior-point solver output. The 2023 paper's featured special case is **univariate polynomials nonnegative over a bounded interval, in several bases**, with a **rational-arithmetic algorithm producing a rational certificate of a WSOS lower bound with a provable bit-size bound**. White's dual is exactly this class (trigonometric / cosine-substituted univariate nonnegativity).
   - Route B (snap-the-primal-Gram, the classic): **Magron et al.**, exact-SOS via perturbation+compensation — **"Exact SOHS decompositions of trigonometric univariate polynomials with Gaussian coefficients"** (arXiv:2202.06544) is the *directly on-point* member: it computes an **exact weighted-sum-of-Hermitian-squares** decomposition for a trigonometric univariate polynomial positive on the unit circle, hybrid numeric→symbolic, with polynomial bit-complexity. This is the literal "Fejér-Riesz exact dual" named as a payoff-class-1 target in `CLAUDE.md` and in companion-memo D2 — now with a concrete algorithm and complexity guarantee.

2. **A far lighter rigor upgrade than the SDPA-GMP serializer exists and is absent from D1–D5: rigorous a-posteriori SDP bounds (Jansson / VSDP).** **Jansson, Chaykin & Keil, "Rigorous Error Bounds for the Optimal Value in Semidefinite Programming"** (SIAM J. Numer. Anal. 46(1), 2007) + the **VSDP-2012** package compute a *verified* lower bound on the SDP optimal value **solely by post-processing the solver's output** (no remodeling), natively handling **LP + SOC + SDP cones** — exactly this project's mixed cone — at small extra cost, and **designed for the ill-conditioned/degenerate/`optimal_inaccurate` regime that the project's whole "reported − last_gap" trick is a hand-rolled substitute for.** This converts CLARABEL's solve into a verified bound without arbitrary-precision re-solving and without the deferred serializer.

3. **The poly-moment infinite-tail rigor caveat (the project's load-bearing #2) has a published, convergence-rate-equipped fix on the torus.** **Henrion & Rudi, "Solving moment and polynomial optimization problems on Sobolev spaces"** (arXiv:2401.07734, 2024) give a moment-SOS hierarchy for nonnegative measures on the unit ball of a Sobolev space of **periodic trigonometric functions**, with **both inner and outer SDP approximations** and **explicit finite-truncation Hausdorff-distance bounds (Prop. 7)** — i.e. a rigorous LB at finite level, the exact discipline the ad-hoc poly-moment tail bound reinvents. Caveat: it lives on the torus, not the interval [−1,1]; adapting it is real work, but it is the principled replacement for the hand-derived tail bound.

4. **No competing LB on µ has appeared; the Erdős LB whitespace is still wide open.** Confirmed via direct search: the only post-White (2022) movement is UB-side AI work (AlphaEvolve 0.380924; TTT-Discover 0.380876; Together 0.380871). This matches the ledger. The autoconvolution-*inequality* LB papers found (arXiv:2508.02803, 2506.16750) are step-function numerical optimizations (UB-style), not proven LBs, and not transferable.

5. **Sister-problem signal worth one paragraph:** White's *own* L² autoconvolution constant μ₂² (**E. P. White, "An optimal L² autoconvolution inequality," Canad. Math. Bull. 2023**) was bounded two-sided to 0.574636066 ≤ μ₂² ≤ 0.574642912 with a *convex* QCLP, and Rechnitzer later pinned it to **128 digits**. The same author's same framework gives a *tight, convergent, two-sided* answer for μ₂² but only a loose-margin one-sided LB for the Erdős µ. The structural reason (μ₂² is an L² = Plancherel-friendly objective; µ is a min-over-translations L^∞-type objective) is exactly why the project's earlier Rechnitzer-transfer attempt was PARTIAL/UB-only — but it also says the *gap* on the Erdős side is a property of the L^∞/minimax structure, not of the framework, which sharpens where a genuinely new idea must bite.

## Cross-domain precedent the project should explicitly borrow: Cohn–Elkies rigor discipline

The sphere-packing LP/SDP-bound community has, for two decades, done *precisely* the thing this project still treats as unsolved: **take a numerically-optimized dual function and turn it into a theorem.** The Cohn–Elkies bound is an infinite-dimensional LP whose *dual-feasible* witness (a function with a verifiable sign/eigenvalue condition) **is** the proof; the numerics only *find* the witness, then exact/interval arithmetic *certifies* it. Recent instances with directly reusable machinery:
- **Cohn–Triantafillou, "Dual linear programming bounds for sphere packing via discrete reductions"** (Sci. Adv. in Math. / arXiv:2206.09876) — a *general method to map an infinite-dimensional dual into a finite, computer-certifiable problem*. This is the abstract template for the project's "ellipse-extension converts per-center duals into a full-space bound" step, done rigorously.
- The Maryland/Cohn-school practice (and Jansson-style interval post-processing) of certifying the found dual in interval arithmetic is the standard "last mile" the project's bound currently skips (its exclusions are "solver-attested, no Farkas cert").

**Why this matters:** the project keeps framing proof-grade rigor as "need SDPA-GMP + serializer" or "need Lean." The sphere-packing precedent shows a *third* established culture — **certify the dual witness directly in exact/interval arithmetic** — which is lighter than both and is exactly what Davis–Papp (Route A above) and Jansson/VSDP operationalize for the SDP setting.

## Whitespace directions (citation-grounded; aimed at THE BAR)

### L1 — Rational dual-certificate snap (Davis–Papp), bypassing the SDPA-GMP serializer (payoff class 1)
**Thesis.** Apply the Davis–Papp dual-certificate construction (arXiv:2305.19039, univariate-on-an-interval case) to White's binding-center dual: take CLARABEL's *dual* solution, and — per their result — build a **rational** WSOS nonnegativity certificate **without rounding or re-projecting a primal Gram matrix and without a special solver**, with a *provable bit-size bound*. Verify dual feasibility in exact arithmetic (sympy/mpmath). The output is a human/Lean-checkable rational certificate of µ ≥ (binding-row LB), independent of any floating-point solver in the trusted base.
**Why whitespace (citation).** D1/D2 in the companion memo assume the only routes to a rational certificate are (i) the unbuilt cvxpy→SDPA-S serializer + GMP re-solve, or (ii) round-the-Gram exact-SOS (Magron-style). Davis–Papp is a *third, lighter* route the project has never cited: it certifies from the **dual** the project already extracts ("rigorous_dual_LB" lives in exactly the dual cone Davis–Papp certify), and its bit-size bound is precisely what makes the certificate finite and checkable. arXiv:2105.11369 (SIAM J. Opt. 2022, foundations) + arXiv:2305.19039 (2023, bit-size + univariate-interval algorithm).
**First step.** Reduce the binding-center dual nonnegativity condition to univariate polynomial nonnegativity on an interval (cosine substitution x=cos θ on White's `cos(πmx/2)` test family); feed the small-N dual vector into a from-scratch implementation of the Davis–Papp rational-certificate algorithm (no public code found — ~1 file, rational LP + interpolation-basis Gram); verify feasibility exactly; report the certified rational LB.
**Payoff class:** 1 (solver-independent, rational, plausibly Lean-checkable certificate). **Effort:** medium (re-implement the algorithm from the paper; no released software). **Fails if:** the *full* augmented dual (Bochner + poly-moment + cell-envelope) doesn't reduce cleanly to a single univariate-interval WSOS instance — mitigate by certifying the **Bochner-only** sub-dual first (a strictly weaker but still-White-beating, fully-exact bound — and it sidesteps the poly-moment caveat, dovetailing with companion-D5); or if the bit size blows up near the thin 5e-5 margin — mitigate by first re-solving the binding center at N≥24000 (already recommended in findings.md) to move off the margin, since bit size scales with inverse distance-to-boundary.

### L2 — Jansson/VSDP rigorous a-posteriori bound on the existing CLARABEL solves (payoff class 1, fastest)
**Thesis.** Replace the project's bespoke "reported − last_gap log-parse" with the **published, verified** Jansson a-posteriori bound: post-process each binding solve (CLARABEL primal/dual + residuals) through the VSDP-style lower-bound formula (interval-arithmetic correction over LP+SOC+SDP cones) to obtain a *rigorously verified* LB on the SDP optimal value at that center — no remodeling, no arbitrary precision, small extra cost. Then the existing adaptive grid+Lipschitz cover (already rigorous) lifts the per-center verified LBs to a verified full-space bound.
**Why whitespace (citation).** Absent from D1–D5 entirely. The companion memo's #1 caveat is "CLARABEL is FP; the LB is `reported − last_gap` parsed from its log — no solver-independent certificate." Jansson–Chaykin–Keil (SIAM J. Numer. Anal. 2007) + VSDP-2012 are *exactly* the verified replacement for that hand-rolled trick, *purpose-built* for the ill-conditioned/degenerate/`optimal_inaccurate` regime, and they handle the mixed cone natively. This is the **cheapest** path to upgrading "numerically certified" → "verified," and it can be applied to the *current* solves without re-architecting anything.
**First step.** Port the VSDP lower-bound post-processing (it's a short, well-documented formula; needs only an interval-arithmetic library — mpmath/`python-flint` available) and run it on one binding-center solve; compare the verified LB to the project's "rigorous_dual_LB" (expect agreement, now *certified* rather than *attested*).
**Payoff class:** 1 (verified bound — solver-independent in the sense that the *bound* is proven from the output by interval arithmetic, not trusted from the solver's status flag). **Effort:** low (post-processing only; no serializer, no GMP re-solve). **Fails if:** the VSDP correction needs a strictly-feasible interior point and the binding instance is too degenerate to supply one — mitigate via Jansson's infeasibility/degeneracy handling (the package explicitly targets this) or perturb the center slightly into the interior and certify the perturbed LB (still valid, marginally weaker). Note: VSDP gives a *verified-interval* certificate, not a *rational* one — for a fully symbolic certificate, chain into L1; L2 is the fast win, L1 is the proof-grade finish.

### L3 — Principled finite-truncation moment-SOS to retire the poly-moment tail-bound caveat (payoff class 1/2)
**Thesis.** Replace the hand-derived poly-moment Hausdorff cuts (rigorous only since the 2026-05-22 IBP tail fix, and the bound's load-bearing seam #2) with the **Henrion–Rudi Sobolev moment-SOS hierarchy** (arXiv:2401.07734), which supplies the *same* positivity content with **published finite-level error bounds (Prop. 7)** and proven inner/outer convergence — i.e. a principled, citable basis for the truncation rather than a one-off remainder estimate. Even short of a full port, the paper's framework certifies *how much* the finite-moment relaxation can be worth, bounding the project's exposure.
**Why whitespace (citation).** The project's poly-moment cuts are the documented sibling of the *retracted* Lasserre lever (both truncate an infinite moment expansion); they survived only by a bespoke tail bound. No prior session connected them to the moment-SOS-with-rigorous-truncation literature. Henrion–Rudi 2024 is the periodic-trigonometric instance with the convergence rates the project needs.
**First step.** Map White's M-side moment constraints onto the torus formulation; check whether the binding-center poly-moment cuts are reproduced (and bounded) by the Henrion–Rudi outer approximation at the project's truncation degree; if so, cite it as the rigor basis and quantify the finite-level slack.
**Payoff class:** 1 (removes/strengthens the weakest rigor seam → contributes to making 0.380284 a theorem) or 2 (if the bounded-interval adaptation yields a clean truncation theorem). **Effort:** medium–high (torus→interval adaptation is the real cost; the paper itself flags non-periodic domains as needing reformulation). **Fails if:** the interval adaptation loses the convergence rate (mitigate: use it only as an *exposure bound* on the existing cuts, not a replacement — still strengthens the rigor narrative), or the rate is too weak at tractable degree to beat the existing IBP bound (then keep the IBP bound, now with literature backing for the *form* of the argument).

### L4 — Trigonometric exact-SOHS (Magron) as the Fejér-Riesz finish for the Bochner block (payoff class 1)
**Thesis.** Use the Magron-school **exact-SOHS-for-trigonometric-univariate-polynomials** algorithm (arXiv:2202.06544) to produce an **exact, rational/Gaussian-integer weighted-sum-of-Hermitian-squares** certificate for the nonnegative trigonometric polynomial that *is* White's dual Bochner/Toeplitz block — the literal "Fejér-Riesz exact dual" payoff-class-1 target named in `CLAUDE.md`. This is companion-memo D2, now with a concrete published algorithm (hybrid numeric→symbolic, complex-root or SDP front-end, compensation back-end) and a **polynomial bit-complexity guarantee**.
**Why whitespace (citation).** D2 proposed "rational LDLᵀ / rational Fejér-Riesz factorization" but cited no method and gave no complexity handle. arXiv:2202.06544 is exactly that method for the trigonometric case, with proven output-size bounds — de-risking the "is the rational Gram low-height enough to be checkable" worry that D2 flagged as its main failure mode.
**First step.** Extract the binding-center dual Bochner block (from CLARABEL or, better, from L2's verified solve); run the paper's Algorithm (root-isolation variant) to get the exact SOHS; check the certified LB and the bit size against the paper's predicted bound.
**Payoff class:** 1 (exact, formally checkable certificate of the Bochner-block contribution). **Effort:** medium (re-implement from paper; couples to L1/L2 for the dual). **Fails if:** the *Bochner-only* certificate alone doesn't beat White once the other (non-SOHS) constraints are handled exactly — but a White-beating Bochner-only exact bound is itself a clean payoff-class-1 result (and dovetails with companion-D5's small-N Lean target).
