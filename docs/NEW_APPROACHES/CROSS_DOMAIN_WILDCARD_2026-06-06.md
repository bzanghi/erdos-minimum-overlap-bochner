# Cross-Domain Wildcard Approaches — Erdős minimum-overlap µ

**Date:** 2026-06-06
**Lens:** cross-domain-wildcard — transplant the winning technique from the *closest solved extremal-constant problem*.
**Status:** PROPOSALS ONLY. Not claims. Each vetted against the ruled-out ledger.

State of play: LB µ ≥ 0.380284 (augmented Fourier-SDP at White's cover), UB µ ≤ 0.380871 (Together), open gap 5.87e-4. The Fourier-SDP lane (White §5 + Bochner-PSD + poly-moment + cover refinement) is saturated with a *proved* asymptotic ceiling C_∞ ≈ 0.380558 (PRO-6 tautological identity). 42–49% of the open gap is "beyond-framework" — provably unreachable by any Bochner-PSD augmentation of White's continuous program. So a value-moving result MUST leave that program.

Two structural facts that drive the choice of analogue:
1. **The problem has a genuine discrete limit object.** µ = lim_n M(n)/n where M(n) is the min over 2-colorings of {1,…,2n} of the max shift-overlap. White/Together both *leave* the integers (continuous step-densities). The discrete-limit lane is essentially unmined for the LB (SAT only computes exact M(n)≤20; no *limit* certificate).
2. **The load-bearing constraint is quadratic-moment, not pointwise.** PRO-22 proved the cell-envelope is necessary because it encodes White's identity ∫M·cos(πmx/2)dx = (4 sin(πm/2)/πm)·a_m − 2(a_m²+b_m²): a **degree-2 relation between the Fourier moments of M and of f=½(1+Σ c cos + d sin)**. That is exactly the algebraic shape that flag-algebra and noncommutative-moment machinery is built to certify — and neither has been pointed at this problem.

## Approach A — Flag-algebra / continuous-combinatorics certificate on the M(n)→µ limit object

**Technique (specific).** Razborov's semidefinite flag-algebra method (continuous combinatorics), in the *ordered/sequence* incarnation (Balogh–Hu–Lidický–Pikhurko–Udvari–Volec for permutations; the "sums of squares for combinatorial limits" framework). The limit object here is a **2-coloring of the integer line** — equivalently a {0,1}-valued function χ on a "sequenceon" (the order-theoretic analogue of a graphon for linearly-ordered ground sets). Encode the overlap functional as a density: for a fixed shift pattern, overlap_k/n → the density d(P_k; χ) of the 3-point ordered pattern "position i colored A, position i+k colored B". The target µ = inf over colorings of sup over shift-scales of these pattern densities. Build the flag SDP: pick a flag type σ (a small ordered colored window), form the moment matrix of σ-flags, impose PSD-ness, and combine with the linear pattern-density identities (chain rule / averaging over the order). The *dual* SDP solution is a **sum-of-squares certificate of σ-flags** that proves a **lower bound** on the achievable max-overlap density — i.e. directly on µ — that holds for *every* coloring, finite or limit, with NO Fourier truncation and NO ellipse cover.

Concretely the object to compute: the level-(N=3 or 4) flag SDP whose value is max_χ … no — for a *min over χ of a sup* you certify via the standard flag inf trick: bound inf_χ sup_k d(P_k;χ) from below by exhibiting flag SOS λ ≥ 0 and a constant c with  Σ_σ λ_σ ⟦q_σ²⟧ + Σ_k (slack_k) ⪰ (d(P_k;χ) − c)  as a flag-algebra identity, certifying d ≥ c for all χ at *some* k. The largest such c is a rigorous LB on µ.

**Citation.** Razborov, *Flag Algebras*, J. Symb. Logic 72 (2007). Balogh, Hu, Lidický, Pikhurko, Udvari, Volec — permutation flag algebras (arXiv:1411.3024; arXiv:1704.02959 "packing densities of 4-point permutations"). "Sums of Squares for Limits of Trees" (arXiv:2404.12838, 2024) and the 2026 proof-system survey arXiv:2601.12741 — show the method is actively being extended to non-graph linearly-structured limits, exactly our regime. IAS "Introduction to Continuous Combinatorics I: the semidefinite method of flag algebras."

**Why untested here (vs ledger).** The ledger's SDP entry is *White's analytic Fourier LP* and its Bochner/poly-moment/Lasserre-on-f augmentations — all relaxations of the **continuous** density f on [0,2]. Flag algebras is an SDP on a *completely different relaxation*: the moment problem of the **discrete-coloring graphon limit**, with pattern densities (not Fourier coefficients) as variables. The "ILP/SAT for exact M(n)" entry computes individual finite M(n) and was ruled *uninformative for µ* precisely because finite n stays ≥0.4 — but flag algebras certifies the n→∞ *limit* directly, which is the quantity that drops to ~0.38. No flag SOS has ever been built for overlap/autocorrelation patterns (the lit covers monotone subsequences, packing densities, Turán). It is neither Bochner-on-f, nor poly-moment, nor Lasserre-level-2-on-f, nor the spectral reformulation.

**First experiment (cheap).** Enumerate all 2-colored ordered windows of size ℓ=4 (16 patterns) and ℓ=5. Write overlap density as a linear functional of size-4 pattern densities (closed form via averaging shift k over the window). Assemble the flag moment matrix for type σ = single colored point (the smallest non-trivial type), giving a tiny SDP (matrices ≤ ~10×10). Solve with CLARABEL/Mosek. Even the level-(ℓ=4) bound is a sanity check: it must satisfy 0.25 ≤ c ≤ 0.5 and ideally beat Erdős' classical 0.293. If the *trend* across ℓ=4→5→6 climbs toward 0.38 it is the first non-Fourier LB ladder and is worth scaling (flag SDPs at ℓ=7–8 are routine on a workstation with `flagmatic`/`sage` + a rational-rounding SDP solver). Code lives naturally beside `_sat_Mn.py`.

**Why it could beat the saturation.** The framework ceiling C_∞≈0.380558 is a theorem *about White's program only*. Flag algebras is a different convergent hierarchy with its *own* limit — there is no reason its ceiling coincides with White's, and flag bounds frequently turn out sharp at low level with the *exact* extremal limit object falling out of the dual (which would also hand us the structure of the true minimizer, currently unknown). Crucially it is **certificate-grade and rational-roundable** (the flag-algebra community routinely produces exact rational SOS certificates), so a success is simultaneously a value improvement AND a fundamentally different, fully-rigorous proof — sidestepping the entire dual-extraction/`optimal_inaccurate` rigor apparatus.

**Risk.** (1) The overlap functional is a *sup over a growing family of shifts k ~ αn* — flag algebras natively handles fixed finite patterns; encoding "max over all linear-scale shifts" may require a continuum of pattern-density constraints (a min-of-sup, harder than the standard max-density problem). The right move is likely to fix the shift-fraction α as a parameter and certify inf_χ d(P_α;χ) for each α, then optimize α — tractable but adds a 1-parameter outer loop. (2) Flag bounds can stall below the truth (e.g. early Turán-density attempts), in which case we get a valid-but-weaker LB — though even matching 0.38 by a non-Fourier route would be a publishable independent confirmation. (3) Engineering: needs a flag-enumeration + SDP-assembly pipeline (flagmatic exists but is sequence/permutation-adapted only in recent forks). Medium build cost (~1–2 weeks to first ℓ=5 number).

## Approach B — Noncommutative moment (NPA / Helton–McCullough) certificate for the operator eigenvalue form sup_t ⟨h, T_t h⟩

**Technique (specific).** Treat µ = 1 − sup_h inf_t ⟨h, T_t h⟩ (PRO-29 duality) as a **noncommutative polynomial eigenvalue-optimization** problem and certify the inner inf_t ⟨h, T_t h⟩ via the Navascués–Pironio–Acín / Helton–McCullough Positivstellensatz hierarchy, NOT the commutative Lasserre-on-f hierarchy that was retracted. The variables are the **shift operators** {T_t} (a one-parameter unitary group: T_s T_t = T_{s+t}, T_t* = T_{−t}) together with the multiplication operator by the density h. The objective ⟨h, T_t h⟩ = ⟨ψ| h† T_t h |ψ⟩ is a noncommutative polynomial in these operators acting on the cyclic vector ψ=1. Build the NPA moment matrix Γ indexed by words in {T_{t_i}, h, h†} up to length 2, impose Γ ⪰ 0 plus the group/commutation localizing constraints (T unitary; h Hermitian with 0 ⪯ h ⪯ 1 — *as operator inequalities*, which is the natural home of the L∞ bound that killed every B-S/M-R transfer) and the normalization ⟨ψ|h|ψ⟩ = 1. The SDP **lower-bounds the minimal eigenvalue** of the relevant operator, hence upper-bounds µ_dual, hence **lower-bounds µ**.

Object to compute: the level-2 NPA relaxation of  min over the operator state of  ⟨ h† (½∫_t w(t) T_t) h ⟩  where w is a probability measure on shifts realizing the inf_t (the inf_t becomes a min over w in the dual, a finite LP layer on top of the NPA SDP). Variables: the moments ⟨word⟩, word length ≤ 4.

**Citation.** Navascués, Pironio, Acín, *A convergent hierarchy of SDPs characterizing the set of quantum correlations*, NJP 2008. Helton–McCullough Positivstellensatz. Pironio–Navascués–Acín, "Convergent relaxations of polynomial optimization with noncommuting variables," SIAM J. Optim. 2010. **arXiv:2402.02126** (2024) "Upper bound hierarchies for noncommutative polynomial optimization" and **arXiv:2510.08427** (2025) "A convergent hierarchy of spectral-gap certificates for qubit Hamiltonians" — both give *eigenvalue* lower bounds via NPA exactly in the form needed here. Operator-inequality 0⪯h⪯1 localizing à la Helton–McCullough–Putinar.

**Why untested here (vs ledger).** The retracted Lasserre attempt and the polynomial-moment Hausdorff cuts are *commutative* moment relaxations of the scalar function f and its Fourier coefficients; they truncate (f²)̂(m) and needed a tail bound (the recurring trap). The NPA hierarchy relaxes the **operator algebra of shifts** — a genuinely noncommutative object (T_t and multiplication-by-h do not commute) — and the truncation is by *word length in the operators*, which is finite-dimensional and tail-free (no infinite Fourier sum to bound). The "spectral/translation-operator min-max reformulation (naive bound 4× loose)" ledger entry used a single **Rayleigh quotient** of one operator sym(A_j) over the *unconstrained* L² ball — NPA is the opposite: it imposes the 0⪯h⪯1 and ‖h‖₁=1 constraints *as operator localizing inequalities inside the moment matrix*, which is exactly the polytope restriction PRO-29 identified as the missing 4× factor. So this transcends rather than re-treads the spectral entry, and is orthogonal to all f-side moment work.

**First experiment (cheap).** Discretize the shift group to a small finite cyclic group Z_m (m=8–16 shift generators), build the level-1 then level-2 NPA moment matrix over words in {T_1,…,T_{m−1}, h} (matrix size grows ~ (#words)²; level-2 with m=8 is ~80×80 — trivial). Impose unitarity of T as linear moment equalities, 0⪯h⪯1 via the localizing matrices [h]·Γ and [1−h]·Γ ⪰ 0, and ⟨h⟩=1. Solve min ⟨h T_k h⟩ jointly over the dual shift-measure. Compare the level-2 LB to (i) the trivial 0.293, (ii) White 0.379. If level-2 already clears 0.30 and *rises* with m and level, the hierarchy is biting where the naive Rayleigh bound failed. ~3–5 days to first number; reuses cvxpy.

**Why it could beat the saturation.** It changes the *certified object* from "Fourier coefficients of a continuous f" to "noncommutative moments of the shift action," whose relaxation gap is unrelated to White's C_∞. The L∞ constraint enters losslessly as an operator inequality (the thing that made every autocorrelation-bracket transfer fail). NPA is known to be *exact at finite level* for many eigenvalue problems with enough symmetry (and our problem has a huge symmetry group: the shift group itself), so finite-level sharpness is plausible. A clean NPA certificate is also fully rigorous at GMP precision and rational-roundable.

**Risk.** (1) The shift group is **continuous** (t ∈ ℝ/2ℤ); discretizing to Z_m introduces an approximation that must be controlled — the honest version needs a Bochner/Naimark argument that the finite-shift NPA bound extends to the continuum (a tail-bound-flavored step — *watch the rigor trap*; but here it bounds a *unitary group character*, which has clean modulus-1 control, unlike the f² Fourier tail). (2) Whether 0⪯h⪯1 + ‖h‖₁=1 as operator localizers actually recovers the polytope tightness, or whether NPA-level-2 is still loose, is unknown a priori — could replicate the 4× looseness if the moment matrix is too small. (3) Existence of a `ncpol2sdpa`/`TSSOS`-style toolchain is required; the encoding is more exotic than cvxpy-native. Medium-high uncertainty, high payoff (a fundamentally new proof route).

## Approach C — Magic-function / Fourier-interpolation certificate at the binding shift set (Viazovska transplant)

**Technique (specific).** Import the **Cohn–Elkies → Viazovska "magic function"** template, but for the *autocorrelation-max* problem rather than sphere packing. The Cohn–Elkies LP bound for packing is certified by a single function g with sign conditions (g ≤ 0 outside the core, ĝ ≥ 0), and Viazovska's breakthrough was *constructing that magic function explicitly* as a ±1-Fourier-eigenfunction built from (quasi)modular forms, making the LP bound **sharp**. The analogue here: White's program is a Fourier LP whose dual optimum is a "certificate function" φ(x) with φ ≥ 0 and prescribed Fourier-side sign/positivity conditions such that ⟨M, φ⟩ lower-bounds sup_t M(t). The current SDP finds φ *numerically* in a fixed trig-polynomial basis (this is the saturating step). The wildcard move: **construct φ analytically** as a structured ±1-eigenfunction of the relevant reflection/Fourier symmetry — i.e. ask whether the extremal certificate is forced to be a specific special function (theta-like / modular-form-like / prolate-spheroidal), giving a *closed-form magic certificate* whose value is exact and whose Fourier positivity is provable by construction (à la Viazovska's modular-form inequalities), bypassing the finite-basis ceiling entirely.

Object to compute: the dual certificate function φ* extracted from a high-precision SDP solve, then run it through **inverse-symbolic / eigenfunction-decomposition analysis** — decompose φ* = α φ₊ + β φ₋ into +1/−1 eigenfunctions of the problem's natural Fourier-type transform (the M↔f autocorrelation symmetry), and test whether φ₊, φ₋ match a one-parameter modular/theta family. The binding shift is the single t*≈33/n (Together's argmax); the magic-function ansatz interpolates φ's prescribed values/derivatives at the discrete binding-shift lattice {t* , 2t*, …} exactly as Viazovska's basis interpolates at √(2k).

**Citation.** Cohn–Elkies, Ann. Math. 157 (2003), arXiv:math/0110009. Viazovska, Ann. Math. 2017 (n=8); Cohn–Kumar–Miller–Radchenko–Viazovska (n=24). Romik / Dan, "Algebraic proof of modular-form inequalities for optimal sphere packings" (arXiv:2406.14659, 2024) and "From sphere packing to Fourier interpolation" (arXiv:2407.14999, 2024) — show how to *prove* the magic-function sign conditions algebraically, the rigor step we'd need. "Fourier inequalities and sign uncertainty" (arXiv:2505.15994, 2024–25) explicitly extends the magic-function machinery to *new* extremal Fourier problems with sign conditions — the live template for transplant.

**Why untested here (vs ledger).** The ledger ruled out "Beurling–Selberg extremal majorants (cancelled)" and "wide-basis PSLQ closed-form hunt for the VALUE µ (negative to 50 digits)." Both are different. Beurling–Selberg builds *majorants of indicators* in a fixed band — a constraint-encoding device, not an attempt to identify the dual certificate as a modular eigenfunction. The PSLQ hunt searched for a closed form of **the constant µ itself** against standard constants — and found none. This approach instead hunts for the closed form of **the dual certificate FUNCTION φ*** (an object with far more structure than a single real number: it has prescribed Fourier support, parity, and interpolation data), using the Viazovska eigenfunction-decomposition + modular-form template that has never been applied to autocorrelation/overlap. It is the *constructive* converse to the SDP, not a constant-fishing expedition.

**First experiment (cheap).** From an existing high-precision row-4 / binding-center solve, extract the dual certificate φ*(x) on a fine grid (the dual variables already give its trig coefficients). (1) Compute its even/odd and ±1-eigenfunction decomposition under x↦−x and under the cos↔transform; (2) tabulate φ*(t*k), φ*'(t*k) at the binding-shift lattice; (3) feed the coefficient sequence and the interpolation data to PSLQ/`mpmath.pslq` and Wolfram inverse-symbolic *as a function* (test against theta-quotients, Eisenstein E₂/E₄/E₆ combinations, prolate-spheroidal wavefunctions for the band [0,2R]). Cheapest possible: it reuses the existing solver output + the PSLQ tooling already in `pslq_hunt.py`. A *hit* — φ* matching a 2–3-parameter modular/theta/prolate family to 30+ digits — would be the entire ballgame.

**Why it could beat the saturation.** The saturation ceiling is a *finite-basis* phenomenon: the trig-polynomial dual can only approach C_∞. A magic function is **not** a trig polynomial — it is an exact entire/special function whose Fourier positivity holds identically, so it escapes the finite-truncation ceiling by construction (exactly how Viazovska beat all numerical LP bounds for packing, which had been stuck for decades). If µ's extremal certificate is special-function-structured, a closed form gives the *exact* µ in one shot and a human-verifiable proof — the strongest possible outcome.

**Risk.** Highest-risk of the three. (1) There is no guarantee the certificate is special-function-structured; sphere packing's magic functions exist because of deep modular symmetry in dimensions 8/24, and µ may simply have an "transcendentally ugly" certificate (the value-PSLQ negative is weak evidence the *function* is ugly too). (2) Even a numerical PSLQ "hit" on the function is not a proof — closing it needs the Romik-style algebraic modular-form-inequality argument, which is months of bespoke special-function work and may not exist for this transform. (3) The interpolation-node structure (binding shift t*≈33/n) is empirical and n-dependent; the clean √(2k) lattice that makes Viazovska work may have no analogue. Treat as a high-variance lottery ticket whose *first experiment is nearly free* (reuses existing solves + PSLQ), so worth one focused probe even though base-rate success is low.

## Cross-cutting note

Ranked by expected value-per-effort:
- **A (flag algebras)** is the strongest wildcard: it is the *canonical* SDP machinery for lower bounds on density-limit minima, it works on the discrete object everyone else abandons, it is certificate-grade/rational-roundable, and its ceiling is provably independent of White's C_∞. Highest chance of a genuinely new, fully-rigorous LB. Main unknown is encoding the sup-over-shift-scales.
- **B (NPA / nc-moment)** is the cleanest fix to the two recurring failures (the L∞ bound enters as an operator inequality; truncation is word-length not Fourier-tail). Medium build, novel proof route, real chance of finite-level sharpness from the shift symmetry.
- **C (magic function)** is a lottery ticket with a near-free first experiment — one PSLQ-on-the-certificate probe reusing existing tooling. Low base rate, but the only route that could yield a *closed-form exact µ*.

None is a re-tread: A relaxes the discrete graphon limit (not White's continuous f); B relaxes the noncommutative shift algebra (not commutative f-moments); C constructs the dual certificate as a special function (not a constant-PSLQ, not a band-limited majorant). All three are explicitly absent from the ruled-out ledger and from `docs/archive/`.
