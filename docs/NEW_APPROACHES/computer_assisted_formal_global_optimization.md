## Framing: what this lens is, and the seam it targets

The project's whole LB apparatus is a **two-point** Fourier/Bochner relaxation: the only correlation object it constrains is the pair autocorrelation `R_h(k) = ⟨h, T_k h⟩`, encoded via `M_n(f) ⪰ 0` on `f` and `1−f`. PRO-22 proved the cell-envelope (White's Parseval identity (W.1)) is load-bearing — it is the *only* link forcing `(a,b)` to come from a genuine `f∈[0,1]`. PRO-6 puts the asymptotic ceiling of this exact apparatus at `C_∞ ≈ 0.380558`. The structural reason the SDP must *relax* rather than solve exactly (LEVER_H_DEEP_READ) is that the L∞ functional `sup_t (h⋆h)(t)` has **no polynomial-in-Fourier-coefficients expansion**, so White is stuck with a two-point PSD outer-approximation.

Three established machineries from the global-optimization / discrete-geometry world attack *exactly that seam* and have **never been applied here** (verified against findings.md, docs/archive/*, and the 2026-06-03 RND_WHITESPACE scan):

- **A.** Three-point / k-point SDP hierarchy (Bachoc–Vallentin / de Laat–Vallentin) — adds the *triple* correlation the two-point relaxation cannot see.
- **B.** A from-scratch **Cohn–Elkies/Delsarte-type LP bound for overlap**, where the dual witness *is* the proof, built natively for this problem rather than as a certification wrapper on White's SDP.
- **C.** **Rigorous interval branch-and-bound** as the *global optimizer* over a finite-dimensional exact reduction (bang-bang structure of the optimizer), with the convex SDP as the per-box bounding oracle.

A crucial disambiguation, because the ledger contains near-namesakes that are NOT these:
- The 2026-06-03 scan cites Cohn–Elkies / Cohn–Triantafillou (arXiv:2206.09876) **only as a "certify-the-dual-witness" discipline** for the *existing* White dual (rigor track). It does **not** propose building a Delsarte-type LP *natively* for overlap (B), nor the k-point hierarchy (A). Different object, different value.
- "CDE" in CDE_PHASE1_RESULT.md = "Constraint **D**iscovery **E**ngine" (cover refinement), **not** Cohn–de Laat–anything. Name collision only.
- L3/Henrion–Rudi moment-SOS in the scan is aimed at retiring the poly-moment *tail-bound caveat* (rigor), not at a measure-relaxation that moves the value.
- Lever I deep-read evaluated **Li 2022** (mis-attributed), concluded "wrong direction for a ceiling theorem." It never evaluated the **k-point primal hierarchy** as a *tightening* of the LB — which is what A is.

## Approach A — Three-point / k-point SDP hierarchy on the overlap autocorrelation

**Technique (the exact object).** White's program constrains only the 2-point correlation. Lift to a **3-point (and then k-point) hierarchy** in the style of Bachoc–Vallentin (kissing numbers, arXiv:math/0608426) and its generalization by de Laat–Vallentin ("A semidefinite programming hierarchy for packing problems in discrete geometry", arXiv:1311.3789) and the k-point equiangular-lines hierarchy (arXiv:1812.06045, *Math. Program.* 2022). Concretely: introduce the triple-overlap statistic
`R³_h(s,t) = ∫ h(x) h(x+s) h(x+t) dx`,
form its moment/Gram matrix in a translation-invariant (Fourier) basis, and impose the SDP positivity that *any genuine* `h∈[0,1]` must satisfy on the 3-point configuration space, simultaneously with the existing 2-point block and the cell-envelope. The objective stays `sup_t M(h,t)`; the new block is a *valid cut* (every real `h` satisfies it), so it can only raise the LB. The natural first rung is the symmetry-reduced 3-point SDP at small Fourier degree, exploiting the dihedral symmetry of the shift torus to block-diagonalize (Bachoc–Vallentin's representation-theory reduction; de Laat's `flagmatic`/`three-point` tooling is open source and reusable).

**Citation.** Bachoc–Vallentin, *J. AMS* 21 (2008) / arXiv:math/0608426 (3-point kissing); de Laat–Vallentin, arXiv:1311.3789 (k-point packing hierarchy, *Math. Program.* 2015); Cohn–Woo / de Laat k-point equiangular lines, arXiv:1812.06045; Musin three-point. Methodologically downstream: Cohn–de Laat–Leijenhorst, "Optimality of spherical codes via exact SDP bounds," arXiv:2403.16874 (2024) — shows k-point SDP duals can be made *exact/sharp* with rational certificates, which matters for closing rather than merely improving.

**Why previously untested (vs ledger).** The entire LB stack is 2-point (Bochner on `f`,`1−f`; poly-moment Hausdorff; Hankel — all single-correlation). PRO-29's spectral reformulation is 2-point. The Barnard–Steinerberger/Madrid–Ramos transfer (PRO-32) is 2-point. Lasserre level-2 (retracted) raised the *moment degree* of the 2-point object, **not the number of points** — a 3-point hierarchy sees configurations (triples `x, x+s, x+t`) that no 2-point relaxation of any degree can express. The ledger's only brush with "three-point" is a one-line mention in OUT_OF_BOX_CROSS_DOMAIN.md §2a flagged as an *open question* and then mis-routed into the Li-2022 ceiling-theorem dead end; the **primal-tightening** use was never built or tested.

**First experiment (cheap).** At small scale `N=300, T=120, Fourier degree R=5`, build the symmetry-reduced 3-point Gram block for `R³_h` over the (s,t) torus (Bachoc–Vallentin reduction → a handful of small PSD blocks), bolt it onto the existing `white_full_convex.build_problem` cone *alongside* the cell-envelope, and solve the row-4 center with CLARABEL. Read two numbers: (i) does the LB rise above the 2-point value at the *same* `N,T,R`? (ii) is the 3-point block *active* (nonzero dual)? If active and the gap to UB shrinks even slightly at toy scale, scale `R` up. Validity sanity-check exactly as PRO-22: reconstruct `f`, verify the reported Ω ≤ true `sup_t (f⋆f)(t)`.

**Why it could beat the SDP-framework saturation.** PRO-6's `C_∞ ≈ 0.380558` ceiling is derived *for the 2-point apparatus* (cell-envelope + Bochner-at-any-n). It is silent about 3-point information because that information is not representable in the 2-point cone — so the ceiling argument simply does not bind the 3-point hierarchy. In sphere packing the jump 2-point (Cohn–Elkies LP) → 3-point (Bachoc–Vallentin SDP) is exactly what broke the LP plateau and gave the best kissing-number bounds; the overlap problem is structurally a packing/energy problem on the shift group, so the same lever should bite. This is the single most principled candidate for pushing past `0.3806`.

**Risk (honest).** (1) Tractability: k-point SDPs are notoriously heavy; even with symmetry reduction the 3-point block at useful `R` may exceed memory before it beats `0.380558`. (2) The triple-overlap cut may be *implied* by the 2-point block + cell-envelope for this specific functional (the overlap objective is intrinsically 2-point — `M(h,t)` only involves pairs), in which case the 3-point block is inactive and gains nothing; the toy-scale "is it active?" probe is designed to kill this fast. (3) Deriving the correct translation-invariant 3-point positivity *and a rigorous truncation/tail bound* for it is real theory (the retracted-Lasserre trap lurks here — any truncated triple-moment expansion needs an analytic remainder, cf. project_tail_bound_rigor_trap.md). Mitigate by first running the *finite* small-N exact version (no tail) to see if there is any gain at all before investing in the tail bound.

## Approach B — A native Cohn–Elkies/Delsarte-type LP bound for the overlap constant (dual witness = proof)

**Technique (the exact object).** Don't augment White's SDP — **re-derive the bound from scratch in the Cohn–Elkies idiom**, where a single explicitly-constructed dual-feasible function *is itself* the proof, with no solver in the trusted base. Cohn–Elkies (Ann. Math. 2003, arXiv:math/0110009) bound sphere-packing density by exhibiting `g` with `ĝ ≥ 0`, `g(x) ≤ 0` for `|x| ≥ r`, `g(0)=ĝ(0)`; the *witness* certifies the bound. The overlap analogue: write the LB problem as an infinite-dimensional LP over the *autocorrelation measure* `ν = ` law of `R_h(·)` (a positive-definite function by Bochner), with linear constraints from `∫h = 1`, `0 ≤ h ≤ 1` (a Hausdorff/Bochner moment cone), and objective `inf_t (1 − R_h(t))`. Its **LP dual** asks for a measure `λ` on shifts `t` and an auxiliary positive-definite `g` such that a one-line inequality `1 − Σ λ_t R(t) ≥ value` holds for *every* admissible `R` — and any such `(λ, g)` proves `µ ≥ value` by a two-line argument, with finite/interval-certifiable hypotheses. The computation: parametrize `g` in a finite trig/Chebyshev basis, solve the finite dual LP to get a numeric witness, then **construct the witness in closed/rational form** and verify its (few) sign + positive-definiteness conditions in exact arithmetic.

**Citation.** Cohn–Elkies, *Ann. Math.* 157 (2003), arXiv:math/0110009; Cohn "A conceptual breakthrough in sphere packing", *Notices AMS* 2017; for the construction-of-witness craft and exactness: de Laat–Dostert–Oliveira, "Exact SDP bounds for packing problems," arXiv:2001.00256; Cohn–de Laat–Leijenhorst arXiv:2403.16874 (2024). For the LP-duality-to-witness template on a *general* extremal functional: Cohn–Triantafillou arXiv:2206.09876 (used here for its *primal LP construction*, NOT — as the rigor track uses it — as a certification wrapper on White's dual).

**Why previously untested (vs ledger).** PRO-22 showed you cannot *drop* White's cell-envelope and keep his Fourier *primal*. Approach B does something different: it **abandons White's primal formulation entirely** and rebuilds the bound as a self-contained LP whose dual witness is the proof — the cell-envelope's job (forcing admissibility) is instead carried by an explicit Bochner/Hausdorff moment-cone in the *new* LP, not patched onto White's. The 2026-06-03 scan invokes Cohn–Elkies only to *certify White's existing dual*; it never proposes a **native overlap-LP** with its own (possibly different, possibly tighter) optimal value. A genuinely different primal ⇒ a genuinely different — and potentially provable-by-hand — bound.

**First experiment (cheap).** Forget rigor first: at degree `D = 8` trig basis, set up the finite *dual* LP (variables = coefficients of `g` + a discrete shift-measure `λ` on a `t`-grid of size 200) directly in cvxpy/scipy.linprog, maximize the certified `value`. Compare its optimum to (i) White's `0.379005`, (ii) the project's `0.380284`, (iii) `C_∞ = 0.380558`. This is a sub-minute LP. The decisive question: **does this native dual reach a value the 2-point SDP cannot, or does it plateau at/below `C_∞`?** Either answer is informative; if it clears `C_∞`, hunt the witness in closed form (PSLQ on the `g`-coefficients — tooling exists in `pslq_hunt.py`).

**Why it could beat the SDP-framework saturation.** Two independent reasons. (i) *Different relaxation, different ceiling*: PRO-6's `C_∞` bounds *White's specific* cell-envelope+Bochner program; a Cohn–Elkies-style LP with a Hausdorff moment cone on `h` directly (rather than the cell-min envelope) is a *different* outer approximation whose optimum is not a priori capped by `0.380558`. (ii) *Proof-grade, not just numeric*: the deliverable is a hand/interval-checkable witness, so if it clears the current bound it is simultaneously a *new value* and a *proof* — exactly the "fundamentally different route" the goal asks for, sidestepping the entire CLARABEL-`dual_extractor` epistemic apparatus.

**Risk (honest).** (1) Most likely failure: the native LP *is* equivalent to (a weakening of) White's program and plateaus at or below `C_∞` — Cohn–Elkies-type LPs are famously *non-sharp* off the magic dimensions (Cohn–de Laat–Salmon proved LP non-sharpness in d=12,16), and overlap may be such a "non-magic" case. The toy-LP probe settles this in minutes. (2) Constructing a closed-form witness that survives exact verification is hard even when the numerics clear the bar (Viazovska-level craft in the worst case); mitigate by accepting an *interval-certified* witness as the deliverable rather than a fully symbolic one. (3) Getting the admissibility cone (Bochner + `0≤h≤1` + `∫h=1`) exactly right in the dual is subtle; a too-loose cone gives an *invalid* (over-strong) bound — must validity-check against the known UB `0.380871` (any claimed LB above it is a bug).

## Approach C — Rigorous interval branch-and-bound over a finite-dimensional exact reduction (SDP as bounding oracle)

**Technique (the exact object).** Replace "trust a floating-point SDP at row centers + Lipschitz cover" with a **rigorous interval branch-and-bound (IB&B) global optimizer** in the αBB / `ICOS` / `Ibex` tradition, run over a *finite-dimensional exact* reduction of the µ problem. Two composable pieces:
1. **Exact finite reduction via optimizer structure.** The inner `sup_t` is attained on a finite shift set, and the overlap optimizer is plausibly *bang-bang* (level-set structure: White/Haugland optimizers are step functions — extreme points of `{0≤h≤1, ∫h=1}` against a `sup` of bilinear forms are `{0,1}`-valued a.e.). If `h` reduces to `K` breakpoints `0=x_0<…<x_K=2`, then `µ = min over breakpoint vectors of max over a finite shift set of M`, a **finite-dimensional minimax** — solvable by rigorous IB&B to a *certified* global minimum (interval arithmetic discards boxes that provably cannot contain the optimum; convergence certifies the gap including rounding error).
2. **SDP/convex relaxation as the per-box lower-bounding oracle.** On each box of breakpoint-space, the αBB convex underestimator (Adjiman–Floudas, *J. Glob. Opt.* 1998) — or White's own SDP restricted to that box — supplies a *rigorous* lower bound on `min_box max_t M`; the cell-envelope is reused as the validity link *inside* each box. This is the standard "convex relaxation as B&B bound" but with the **exact** minimax as the target, not a single point.

**Citation.** Adjiman–Androulakis–Floudas αBB, *J. Glob. Opt.* / *AIChE J.* 1998 (convex underestimators for nonconvex global optimization); Lebbah–Michel–Rueher `Quad`/`ICOS` rigorous global optimization, *Optim. Methods Softw.* 24 (2009); Ninin–Messine–Hansen rigorous affine-relaxation B&B; `Ibex` (Chabert–Jaulin) interval solver; benchmark precedent "Certified Global Minima for a Benchmark of Difficult Optimization Problems," arXiv:2003.09867. For the bang-bang/extreme-point structural claim: standard infinite-dimensional LP / Choquet theory; cf. White (2023) and Haugland step-function optimizers as empirical evidence.

**Why previously untested (vs ledger).** The project's rigor *is* a cover argument: solve the SDP at fixed centers, then a Lipschitz/`eps_grid` envelope over `(h,p,q)` boxes (FULLSPACE memos). That is **single-point solve + passive cover**, not an *active* B&B that *branches on the optimizer's own degrees of freedom* and *proves global optimality*. RND_WHITESPACE D5 is "exact small-N SDP at one center" — still a single SDP, not B&B. No memo branches on breakpoint-space or uses αBB underestimators; "interval arithmetic" appears in the ledger **only** as Jansson/VSDP *post-processing of one SDP solve* (L2) — the certification track, the opposite of using IB&B as the *search*. The bang-bang reduction of the *continuous* optimizer to a finite minimax was never exploited (LEVER_D refuted *small-n discrete lifts converging to Together's h\**, a different claim: that empirical optimizers have O(1) breakpoints — it did NOT test a rigorous B&B over a *fixed* breakpoint count as a global *lower* bound).

**First experiment (cheap).** Fix a coarse breakpoint count `K = 6` and a finite shift grid. Implement the finite minimax `min_{0≤h_i≤1, Σ=1} max_{t∈grid} M(h,t)` and run it through a rigorous IB&B (use `Ibex`/`ICOS` if installable, else a 1-file natural-interval-extension B&B with the αBB quadratic underestimator) to get a *certified* global-min interval `[lo, hi]` for `K=6`. Two reads: (i) is `lo` already above White's `0.379005` at tiny `K`? (any certified `lo` is an honest LB on the `K`-restricted problem — and if the restriction is *relaxational* (lower-bounding) rather than restrictive, on `µ` itself). (ii) How does `lo` move as `K: 6→8→10`? A monotone climb toward `0.3806+` with certified intervals would be a fundamentally different — fully verified, no CLARABEL-log-parse — route to the bound.

**Why it could beat the SDP-framework saturation.** The saturation ceiling is a property of *one convex relaxation evaluated once per center*. IB&B is not bounded by any single relaxation's gap: it *closes* the gap by exhaustion of the (finite-dimensional) search space, using the convex bound only to *prune*. Where the SDP framework asymptotes at `C_∞` because its relaxation gap to `µ` is bounded below, B&B's certified interval `[lo,hi]` can in principle be driven to `µ` itself as boxes shrink — the relaxation gap controls *speed*, not the *limit*. And the output is a verified interval, so a successful run both *changes the value* and *is the proof* (no separate rigor track needed).

**Risk (honest).** (1) Curse of dimensionality is brutal: rigorous IB&B is exponential in the number of branched variables; `K` breakpoints means `~2K` variables, and useful `K` (Together's h\* has 400+ steps) is hopeless for exhaustive B&B — the bet is that a *small* `K` already gives a strong *lower* bound (the `K`-step problem lower-bounds `µ` iff one sets it up as a relaxation, which requires care). (2) The bang-bang/finite-breakpoint reduction must be proven to *lower-bound* `µ` (an over-restriction would give an *upper* bound on a sub-problem, not a valid LB on `µ`) — getting the relaxation direction right is the crux and the main way this silently produces garbage; validity-check every certified `lo` against `0.380871`. (3) Even with αBB the per-box bound may be too loose to prune effectively, so the tree never closes at useful `K` — mitigate by using White's *SDP* (tighter than αBB) as the box oracle, accepting that each node is then an SDP solve (slow but rigorous).

## Cross-cutting note

A — k-point hierarchy — is the highest-leverage *value* play (most likely to crack `0.3806` if the triple block is active), and is the natural "transcend, don't re-tread" combination of the ruled-out Lasserre-degree idea (it raises *points*, not *degree*). B — native Cohn–Elkies LP — is the highest-leverage *proof-route* play (deliverable is a witness, bypassing the entire `dual_extractor` epistemics) and has the cheapest decisive probe (a sub-minute LP that says go/no-go vs `C_∞`). C — rigorous IB&B — is the most computer-assisted-formal in spirit (certified global optimization, gap-closing-by-exhaustion rather than relaxation-bounded) and the only one whose limit is `µ` itself rather than a relaxation ceiling, at the cost of the harshest tractability wall.

All three share the same first-line validity guard the project already learned from PRO-22: **reconstruct and check against the known UB `0.380871`** — any proposed LB above it is a bug in the relaxation direction, not a result.

**Recommended order of cheap probes:** B's toy dual LP (minutes, settles "is a different primal even capable of beating `C_∞`?") → A's "is the 3-point block active at toy scale?" (the structural make-or-break for the value play) → C's `K=6` certified interval (proof-of-concept for the verified-search route).
