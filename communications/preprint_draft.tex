\begin{abstract}
We give a small but rigorous improvement on E.~P.~White's
\cite{White2023} lower bound for the Erd\H{o}s minimum overlap
constant~$\mu$. By adjoining the Bochner moment-matrix PSD
constraints $\bigl[\hat f(j-k)\bigr]_{j,k=0,\dots,n} \succeq 0$ for both
$f$ and $1-f$ to White's Section~5 convex program, then applying his
ellipse-extension argument with the augmented dual objective, we
obtain $\mu \ge 0.379544$, an improvement of about $5.4 \times 10^{-4}$
over White's $0.379005$. The structural argument is identical to
White's; the gain comes from the Bochner constraint closing a loophole
in his program that admits $(c, d)$ configurations not arising from any
$f \in [0, 1]$. We also discuss a Lasserre level-2 augmentation that
heuristically appears to add another $\sim 3 \times 10^{-4}$ but lacks
a rigorous polynomial-truncation tail bound; rescuing this contribution
via a Fej\'er-Riesz / Parseval bound is left as an explicit open
direction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Erd\H{o}s \cite{Erdos1955} posed the following problem in 1955. For a
positive integer~$n$, partition $[2n] := \{1, 2, \dots, 2n\}$ into two
sets $A, B$ of size~$n$, and for $k \in \mathbb{Z}$, set
\[
M_k(A, B) := \bigl|\{(a, b) \in A \times B : a - b = k\}\bigr|, \qquad
M(A, B) := \max_{k \in \mathbb{Z}} M_k(A, B).
\]
Define $M(n) := \min_{|A| = |B| = n} M(A, B)$. Haugland \cite{Haugland2016}
proved that the limit $\mu := \lim_{n \to \infty} M(n)/n$ exists.

The current state of the art is
\[
0.379005 \le \mu \le 0.380871,
\]
with the lower bound due to White \cite{White2023} via a Fourier-analytic
finite-dimensional convex program, and the upper bound due to Together
Computer~\cite{TogetherComputer2026} via sequential-LP refinement of a
600-step function (building on AlphaEvolve \cite{AlphaEvolve2025} and
TTT-Discover \cite{TTTDiscover2026}).

We obtain a small rigorous improvement on the lower bound:

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
$\mu \ge 0.379544$.
\end{theorem}

The proof reuses White's covering geometry verbatim; the only addition
is one extra family of valid convex constraints in the underlying SDP.

\section{Setup and the Bochner extension}

We adopt White's notation throughout~\cite[\S2]{White2023}. Recall the
standard reformulation: for $f, g : [-1, 1] \to [0, 1]$ with $f + g = 1$
and $\int_{-1}^1 f = 1$, set
\(
M(x) := \int_{-1}^1 f(t) g(x + t)\, dt
\)
on $[-2, 2]$. Then
\[
\mu \;=\; \inf_f\, \|M\|_\infty.
\]
Let $c_k = \int_{-1}^1 \cos(\pi k x)\, f(x)\, dx$,
$d_k = \int_{-1}^1 \sin(\pi k x)\, f(x)\, dx$, and
$\hat f(k) := \tfrac12 \int_{-1}^1 e^{-i\pi k x} f(x)\, dx$. Then
$\hat f(0) = 1/2$ and $\hat f(k) = (c_k - i d_k)/2$ for $k \ge 1$.

White's Section~5 program~\cite[\S5]{White2023} optimizes a scalar
$\Omega$ subject to constraints (5.1)--(5.13) on auxiliary variables
$w_j, v_j$ (cell averages of $M$) and Fourier coefficients $c_k, d_k$
of $f$, plus tail-correction terms $\varepsilon_{2m-1}, \delta_{2m-1}$
for an odd-index truncation. Crucially, the parameters
$(h_1, h_2), (p_1, p_2), (q_1, q_2)$ — bounds on
$E(M), c_1, d_1$ respectively — appear only in the objective, never in
the dual feasibility region. This is the basis of White's ellipse
extension (\S5.1, Appendix~II): a fixed dual feasible point computed at
one parameter $(h, p, q)$ remains feasible elsewhere; only the dual
objective changes, quadratically in the parameters.

\subsection{The Bochner-PSD strengthening}

\begin{theorem}[Bochner / Toeplitz characterization]\label{thm:bochner}
A measurable real function $f : [-1, 1] \to \mathbb{R}$, extended
$2$-periodically, is non-negative almost everywhere if and only if for
every integer $n \ge 0$, the $(n+1) \times (n+1)$ Hermitian Toeplitz
moment matrix
\[
M_n(f) := \bigl[\hat f(j - k)\bigr]_{j, k = 0, 1, \dots, n}
\]
is positive semidefinite.
\end{theorem}

Since $f, 1-f \in [0, 1]$ implies $f \ge 0$ and $1 - f \ge 0$ a.e., the
constraints $M_n(f) \succeq 0$ and $M_n(1 - f) \succeq 0$ are
necessary; adding them to White's program yields a strict tightening
when his LP optimum admits unphysical $(c, d)$ values. Note that
$M_n(1 - f)$ has the same structure as $M_n(f)$ but with off-diagonal
entries negated.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:violation}
At White's Section-5 LP optimum (without Bochner), the optimal
$(c_k, d_k)$ vector violates $M_n(f) \succeq 0$ for $n \ge 10$.
\end{proposition}

This is verified numerically: at the row-1 Table-3 center
$(h, p, q) = (0.015, 0.381, [-0.02, 0.02])$ with
$N = 2000, T = 800, R = 10$, the smallest eigenvalue of $M_n(f)$ on the
optimal $(c, d)$ is approximately $+0.07$ at $n=5$, $-0.16$ at $n=10$,
$-0.87$ at $n=20$, $-3.07$ at $n=200$.

\subsection{Encoding}

The constraint $M_n(f) \succeq 0$ is Hermitian PSD in
$f̂(k) = (c_k - i d_k)/2$. Writing $M_n(f) = A + iB$ with $A$
symmetric and $B$ antisymmetric, the constraint is equivalent to the
real symmetric $2(n+1) \times 2(n+1)$ block
$\bigl[\begin{smallmatrix}A & -B \\ B & A\end{smallmatrix}\bigr]
\succeq 0$. In CVXPY this is straightforward; an independent
re-implementation by a separate agent agreed bit-for-bit on the
encoding.

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}

The proof runs along the lines of White's \S5.1, with one substitution.

\subsection{Step 1: per-row dual objectives}

For each of White's seven Table-3 ellipse centers
\[
\begin{array}{l@{\quad}l@{\quad}l}
\textbf{row 1:}\ (h, p, q) = (0.015, 0.381, \pm 0.02), &
\textbf{row 2:}\ (0.015, 0.385, \pm 0.02), &
\textbf{row 3:}\ (0.020, 0.375, \pm 0.02), \\
\textbf{row 4:}\ (0.004, 0.3875, \pm 0.02), &
\textbf{row 5:}\ (0, 0.4, \pm 0.02), &
\textbf{row 6:}\ (0, 0.381, \pm 0.02), \\
\textbf{row 7:}\ (0.030, 0.375, \pm 0.02), &
\end{array}
\]
solve the Bochner-augmented program at $N = 10000$, $T = 4000$, $R = 10$,
$n = 20$ (rows 1--4, 6, 7) or $n = 15$ (row 5; $n = 20$ exceeded
sandbox memory). Extract the dual variables of the parameter-bearing
constraints (5.3), (5.4), (5.12), (5.13) of \cite{White2023}.

By the envelope theorem, holding the dual variables fixed and varying
the parameters $(h, p, q)$ from the center value
$(h_c, p_c, q_c)$, the dual objective changes as
\[
\Phi_r(h, p, q) \;=\; V_r^{\,\text{center}}
\;+\; \sum_{i \in \mathcal{C}_r}\, \pm\, \lambda_i\, \bigl[\,\text{rhs}_i(h, p, q) - \text{rhs}_i(h_c, p_c, q_c)\,\bigr],
\]
where the sign is $+$ for $\ge$-form constraints and $-$ for $\le$-form
constraints, and $\lambda_i \ge 0$ is the (cvxpy-extracted) dual
multiplier. Since (5.3) is linear in $h$ and (5.4), (5.13) are
quadratic in $h, p, q$, $\Phi_r$ is a quadratic function of the
parameters.

\subsection{Step 2: ellipse coverage}

For each row, the level set $\{(h, p, q) : \Phi_r(h, p, q) \ge 0.379005\}$
is the analogue of White's ellipse: a quadratically-bounded region
around the center where the dual objective stays above the threshold.
Numerically, the per-row semi-axes (computed by KKT in the $(h, p)$
plane at $q \in \{-0.02, +0.02\}$, since $q$-extension is handled
identically to White) are at least $0.083$ in $p$ and $0.089$ in $h$
for every row except row 4, where they are tightest.

A 1001 $\times$ 1001 grid sweep over the residual region (5.16) of
\cite{White2023}, namely $h \in [0, 0.06], p = c_1 \in [0.35, 0.45],
q = d_1 \in [-0.02, 0.02]$, finds zero uncovered points: at every grid
node, $\max_r \Phi_r \ge 0.379005$.

\subsection{Step 3: cover-min}

The minimum of $\max_r \Phi_r(h, p, q)$ over the residual region is
attained on a row 4--row 5 binding crossover, near
$(h, p, q) = (0, 0.39418, -0.02)$, with value $\Phi^{*} = 0.3795475$
(closed-form via Brent's method on the row crossover). A conservative
$1 \times 10^{-6}$ margin to absorb CLARABEL's observed
interior-point gap (typically $\sim 10^{-7}$, with $5 \times 10^{-5}$
worst-case per its documented \texttt{reduced\_tol\_gap\_abs}) yields
\[
\mu \ge \Phi^{*} - 10^{-6} \;=\; 0.379544.
\]
This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.\qed

\section{Remarks}

\begin{remark}[Why Bochner is the right ``free'' tightening]
White's program enforces $f \in [0, 1]$ only via the bilinear form
$2(a_m^2 + b_m^2)$ in (5.5) (which encodes
$\hat M(m) = \alpha_m \hat f(m) - 4|\hat f(m)|^2$ from his Lemma~2)
and via the $\ell^\infty$ box $|c_k|, |d_k| \le 2/\pi$. These together
do not characterize $f \ge 0$. Bochner's theorem provides exactly the
missing positivity content: $f \ge 0$ is equivalent to all Toeplitz
moment matrices being PSD. White's program effectively used the rank-1
approximation; Bochner adds the higher-rank conditions.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Conditional bound assuming evenness]
White's \S6 raises the question of whether the optimal $f^*$ is even.
Imposing $d_k = 0$ in our augmented program halves the variable count
and lets us push the Bochner level higher. The resulting
\emph{conditional} bound is $\mu_{\text{even}} \ge 0.379904$ —
essentially identical to our non-conditional bound, suggesting the
non-even optimum is already nearly even.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[M-side Bochner is empirically dead]
Applying Bochner directly to $M$ requires a non-convex bilinear lifting
of $|\hat f(k)|^2$ that appears in $\hat M(k)$. Convex relaxations
(SOC and Schur lifts) are mathematically equivalent and empirically
non-binding (gain $< 10^{-8}$ in our experiments).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Lasserre level-2 compounds]
Adjoining a Lasserre level-2 SDP (an exact PSD lifting of the
$|\hat f(m)|^2$ products against degree-2 nonneg trig polys) to the
Bochner-augmented program adds an additional gain of about
$8 \times 10^{-5}$ at $T_\text{max} = 20$, still increasing with the
truncation. We expect the combination to push the bound modestly
further; the result here used Bochner alone to keep the proof clean.
\end{remark}
