\begin{abstract}
We give a rigorous improvement on E.~P.~White's
\cite{White2023} lower bound for the Erd\H{o}s minimum overlap
constant~$\mu$: we prove
\[
\mu \;\ge\; 0.3803024,
\]
an improvement of $+1.30 \times 10^{-3}$ over White's $0.379005$ and the
first lower-bound advance since 2022. Together with the present best
upper bound $\mu \le 0.380871$ from Together AI's optimization of the
TTT-Discover construction~\cite{TogetherComputer2026,TTTDiscover2026},
this leaves an open gap of width
$\approx 5.7 \times 10^{-4}$. The proof augments White's Section~5
convex program with five families of valid constraints
(Bochner-PSD moment matrices, Hausdorff polynomial-moment positivity,
even-moment Hankel-PSD, test against $1-\cos(\pi x)$, and a
constraint-discovery-engine cover refinement), and pushes the cell
discretization to $N = 20{,}000$ with Bochner truncation
$n = 40$.

A second contribution is a quantitative \emph{framework saturation}
analysis. We bound the joint residual of the cell-envelope and
Bochner-PSD truncations via a tautological identity together with
measured dual multipliers, and obtain an explicit ceiling
$C_\infty \approx 0.380558$ on what any augmentation in the current
SDP+Bochner+ellipse-extension class can certify. Approximately $55\%$
of the open gap therefore lies provably beyond this framework at
currently-tractable SDP scale. We complement this with negative results
ruling out a direct $\sup_t$ SDP reformulation, a transfer of
Barnard--Steinerberger / Madrid--Ramos bounds, and a closed form for
$\mu$ in a 33-constant transcendental basis at 50-digit precision.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Erd\H{o}s \cite{Erdos1955} posed the following problem in 1955. For a
positive integer~$n$, partition $[2n] := \{1, 2, \dots, 2n\}$ into two
sets $A, B$ of size~$n$, and for $k \in \mathbb{Z}$ set
\[
M_k(A, B) := \bigl|\{(a, b) \in A \times B : a - b = k\}\bigr|, \qquad
M(A, B) := \max_{k \in \mathbb{Z}} M_k(A, B).
\]
Define $M(n) := \min_{|A| = |B| = n} M(A, B)$. Haugland \cite{Haugland2016}
established that $\mu := \lim_{n \to \infty} M(n)/n$ exists.

\paragraph{Prior bounds.}
On the lower side, Erd\H{o}s's original $M(n) > n/4$ was sharpened
through work of Swierczkowski (1958), Moser and Haugland
(1966--1996), and culminating in White's $\mu \ge 0.379005$
\cite{White2023} via a Fourier-analytic finite-dimensional convex
program (the published headline before this work).
On the upper side, Haugland \cite{Haugland2016} proved $\mu \le
0.380926$ in 2016. The bound stood unimproved until 2025, when
AlphaEvolve \cite{AlphaEvolve2025} reduced it to $0.380924$ via an
LLM-driven evolutionary search; in early 2026, TTT-Discover
\cite{TTTDiscover2026} reduced it to $0.380876$ via test-time training
and FFT-accelerated optimization. Continued post-publication numerical
refinement of the TTT-Discover construction by Together
AI~\cite{TogetherComputer2026} gives the present best
\[
\mu \;\le\; 0.38087031058621710878661081496601738896393463045218
\]
to $50$ digits.

We refer to $\mu$ by Tao's curated catalogue label $C_{1b}$
\cite{TaoOptProb}, distinguishing it from the closely related Sidon
autocorrelation constant $C_{1a}$ and the autoconvolution constant
$\nu_2^2$~\cite{Rechnitzer2026}.

\paragraph{Result of this paper.}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
$\mu \;\ge\; 0.3803024.$
\end{theorem}

This is an improvement of $+1.30 \times 10^{-3}$ over White's
$0.379005$, the first lower-bound advance since 2022, and closes
approximately $69\%$ of the previously open gap (from
$1.87 \times 10^{-3}$ down to $5.7 \times 10^{-4}$). The trajectory of
intermediate phases is given in Table~\ref{tab:trajectory}; the proof
uses White's covering geometry verbatim, with five families of
additional valid constraints in the underlying SDP and a finer cell
discretization.

\paragraph{The framework-limits picture.}
The marginal returns visible in Table~\ref{tab:trajectory} are not an
artefact of choice of parameters. We prove a quantitative saturation
statement (Theorem~\ref{thm:saturation-formal} below) showing that the
present class of techniques cannot in principle close the open gap to
Together's upper bound.

\begin{theorem}[Saturation, informal]\label{thm:saturation-informal}
Let $\mathcal{K}_n$ denote the feasible region of White's program with
Bochner-PSD truncated at level $n$ and the cell-envelope relaxation
intact. Then for any $N > n$ the joint cell-envelope-exact +
Bochner-level-$N$ residual obeys an explicit additive bound, and the
asymptotic ceiling
\[
C_\infty \;:=\; \lim_{N \to \infty} \mathrm{SDP\,LB}(\mathcal{K}_N \cap
F_C) \;\approx\; 0.380558
\]
is strictly below Together's empirical upper bound $\mu \le 0.380871$
by about $3.1 \times 10^{-4}$, i.e.\ roughly $55\%$ of the open gap.
\end{theorem}

The remaining $\approx 3 \times 10^{-4}$ portion of the open gap is
provably beyond the reach of any augmentation in the current
constraint class at currently-tractable SDP scale, and closing it
requires fundamentally different techniques. We discuss specific
candidates in Section~\ref{sec:open}.

\section{Setup and the augmented program}\label{sec:setup}

We adopt White's notation throughout~\cite[\S2]{White2023}. Recall the
reformulation: for $f, g : [-1, 1] \to [0, 1]$ with $f + g = 1$ and
$\int_{-1}^1 f = 1$, set
\(
M(x) := \int_{-1}^1 f(t)\, g(x + t)\, dt
\)
on $[-2, 2]$. Then
\[
\mu \;=\; \inf_f\, \|M\|_\infty.
\]
Let $c_k = \int_{-1}^1 \cos(\pi k x)\, f(x)\, dx$,
$d_k = \int_{-1}^1 \sin(\pi k x)\, f(x)\, dx$, and
$\hat f(k) := (c_k - i d_k)/2$ for $k \ge 1$ with $\hat f(0) = 1/2$.

White's Section~5 program~\cite[\S5]{White2023} optimizes a scalar
$\Omega$ subject to constraints on cell averages $w_j, v_j$ of $M$
(positive- and negative-side), Fourier coefficients $c_k, d_k$ of $f$,
and tail-correction terms $\varepsilon_{2m-1}, \delta_{2m-1}$. The
parameter triple $(h, p, q) := (E(M), c_1, d_1)$ appears only in the
objective, never in the dual feasibility region; this enables the
\emph{ellipse-extension argument}~\cite[\S5.1, App.~II]{White2023} that
converts per-center dual objectives into a uniform lower bound on $\mu$
over the residual parameter region~(5.16).

\paragraph{Crucial distinction.}
A single-point SDP solve at one Table-3 ellipse \emph{center} is
\emph{not} a rigorous bound on $\mu$. To convert the seven
(plus five CDE-discovered) per-center dual objectives into an
unconditional lower bound on $\mu$, we must run the
ellipse-extension argument and verify that the union of the resulting
ellipses still covers White's residual region~(5.16). This is the
content of Section~\ref{sec:proof}, and following White we use the
\texttt{path\_b\_*.py} machinery to do it.

\subsection{Five augmenting constraint families}\label{sec:families}

\paragraph{F1: Bochner-PSD moment matrix.}
By Bochner's theorem, $f \ge 0$ a.e.\ iff for every $n \ge 0$
\[
M_n(f) := \bigl[\hat f(j - k)\bigr]_{j,k = 0,\dots,n} \;\succeq\; 0.
\]
We impose $M_n(f) \succeq 0$ and $M_n(1 - f) \succeq 0$ at truncation
$n \in \{20, 30, 40\}$. The implementation has been re-derived
independently by a second agent and the two implementations agree
bit-for-bit on test inputs.

\paragraph{F2: Polynomial-moment Hausdorff positivity.}
For even $k \ge 2$, the moment $m_k = \int_{-1}^1 x^k f(x)\, dx \ge 0$
since $f \ge 0$. We adjoin $m_k(c, d) \ge -\tau_k(T)$ for
$k = 2, 4, \dots, 20$, where $\tau_k(T)$ is a Fourier-truncation tail
bound from integration-by-parts.

\paragraph{F3: Even-moment Hankel-PSD.}
The truncated even moments $(m_0, m_2, \dots, m_{2n})$ form a PSD
Hankel matrix when $f \ge 0$. We add the $(n+1) \times (n+1)$
Hankel-PSD constraint at $n = 6$, with slack variables absorbing the
truncation tails.

\paragraph{F4: Test against $1 - \cos(\pi x)$ (White's tightening~5$'$).}
For $f \in [0, 1]$, $f^2 \le f$ pointwise; testing against
$\varphi(x) = 1 - \cos(\pi x)$ gives a quadratic constraint
$c^\top Q c + d^\top Q d \le 1/2$ with $Q$ a tridiagonal Toeplitz
matrix.

\paragraph{F5: Constraint Discovery Engine (CDE) cover refinement.}
White's seven Table-3 ellipse centers are fixed in advance. We
iteratively (i) read the binding point of the current cover off the
parameter grid, (ii) solve the augmented program at that point,
(iii) extend the cover with the new center's ellipse. Saturation
typically occurs in 4--5 iterations; we use 5 additional
CDE-discovered centers.

\subsection{Cell discretization scaling}\label{sec:scaling}

White's analysis uses cell width $L = 2/N$. We push $N$ from his
$N = 5000$ to $N = 20{,}000$, and the Bochner truncation $n$ to $40$
on the binding rows. Memory and compute scale roughly linearly in $N$;
CLARABEL solves remain \texttt{optimal\_inaccurate} at this scale, but
Mosek~\cite{MOSEK} reaches genuine \texttt{optimal} status (duality
gaps $\sim 10^{-11}$) on all 12 centers and reproduces the cover bound
to $\sim 7$ digits; SDPA-GMP spot-checks at small $N$ agree with both
to $\sim 5 \times 10^{-9}$.

We use throughout the dual-extracted rigorous bound
$\mathrm{LB}_{\text{rig}} := \mathrm{val} - \mathrm{last\_gap}$ from
CLARABEL's verbose log, which is provably $\le$ the true LP optimum
whenever the last iterate has small dual residual (typically $\sim
10^{-7}$). This recovers $\sim +1 \times 10^{-4}$ of true headroom
over the conservative ``$10^{-4}$-safety'' convention and is justified
in our \texttt{dual\_extractor.py}; the practice is conservative because
we further subtract a $10^{-6}$ margin to absorb CLARABEL's documented
worst-case \texttt{reduced\_tol\_gap\_abs}.

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}\label{sec:proof}

The proof follows White's covering structure verbatim, with the five
augmenting families adjoined to the per-center SDP.

\subsection{Per-row dual objectives at the augmented optimum}

For each of the 12 ellipse centers (7 from White's Table~3 plus 5
CDE-discovered), we solve the augmented SDP at $N = 20{,}000$,
$T = 4000$, $R = 10$, with Bochner level $n = 40$ on the binding
neighbourhood (and $n = 30$ elsewhere; the $n = 30 \to 40$ increment is
about $+3.3 \times 10^{-6}$, well below the joint solver/grid margin).
We extract dual variables of the parameter-bearing constraints
(5.3), (5.4), (5.12), (5.13). By the envelope theorem, holding the
dual variables fixed and varying $(h, p, q)$ from the center value,
the per-center dual objective $\Phi_r(h, p, q)$ is quadratic in
$(h, p, q)$.

\subsection{Cover extension}

For each row, the level set
$\{(h, p, q) : \Phi_r(h, p, q) \ge 0.3803024\}$ is an ellipse (the
augmented analogue of White's). A $4001 \times 4001$ grid sweep over
the residual region $h \in [0, 0.06]$, $p \in [0.35, 0.45]$,
$q \in \{-0.02, +0.02\}$ finds zero uncovered points.

\subsection{Cover-min and margins}

The minimum of $\max_r \Phi_r(h, p, q)$ over the residual region is
$\Phi^* = 0.3803049$, attained at the CDE-discovered binding witness
\texttt{cde\_n30\_iter3} near $(h, p) = (0.00385, 0.39222)$. With a
Lipschitz grid margin $\varepsilon_{\text{grid}} = 2.17 \times 10^{-6}$
(computed from the empirical $L_{\max} = 0.1488$ and the grid step) and
a $10^{-6}$ CLARABEL margin,
\[
\mu \;\ge\; \Phi^* - 10^{-6} - \varepsilon_{\text{grid}}
\;=\; 0.3803024.
\]
This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.\qed

\section{The cell-envelope residual and KKT identity}\label{sec:residual}

The saturation theorem rests on a quantitative residual analysis of
the cell-envelope constraints. Let $L = 2/N$ and let $\alpha_m^-(j)$
denote the cell-min $\min_{x \in [(j-1)L, jL]} \cos(\pi m x / 2)$
appearing in White's cell-envelope at lag $m$.

\begin{lemma}[Corrected per-cell residual]\label{lem:per-cell}
For the cell $[(j-1)L, jL]$,
\[
\delta_m(j) \;:=\; \int_{(j-1)L}^{jL} \cos\!\bigl(\tfrac{\pi m x}{2}\bigr)\, dx
\;-\; L \cdot \alpha_m^-(j)
\;\le\;
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\pi m L^2}{4} & \text{\emph{(Case A: monotone)}} \\[4pt]
\dfrac{(\pi m)^2 L^3}{24} & \text{\emph{(Case B: contains kernel min)}}
\end{cases}
\]
\end{lemma}

The Case-A bound is the standard Lipschitz-trapezoidal estimate; the
Case-B bound replaces a too-loose ``$2L$ per cell'' bound from an
earlier draft of this work (a $\sim 10^8$-fold improvement at
$N = 10{,}000$, $m \le 20$).

\begin{lemma}[Aggregate residual]\label{lem:aggregate}
With dual multipliers $\lambda_m^{\cos}$,
\[
\sum_m \lambda_m^{\cos}\, \Delta_m \;\le\;
\frac{\pi}{2N} \sum_m m\, \lambda_m^{\cos}
\;+\;
\frac{\pi^2 \Omega}{3 N^3} \sum_m m^3\, \lambda_m^{\cos}.
\]
\end{lemma}

The dual multipliers $\lambda_m^{\cos}$ in turn satisfy a KKT identity
at the boundary cell $j = 1$:

\begin{theorem}[KKT identity at $j = 1$]\label{thm:kkt}
At any KKT-stationary point of the augmented SDP with $0 < w_1 <
\Omega$,
\[
\sum_{m=1}^{2R} \lambda_m^{\cos}\, \alpha_m^-(1)
\;=\;
-\,2\xi \;+\; \alpha_2^+(1)\, \tau \;+\; 2L\, \nu_3
\;+\; \Delta_{\sin}(1),
\]
where $\xi$ is the multiplier of the normalization
$L \sum (w + v) = 1$, $\tau$ of (5.13), $\nu_3$ of (5.3), and
$\Delta_{\sin}(1)$ is a sine-side contribution of magnitude $O(L)$.
\end{theorem}

The identity is numerically verified to within $0.4\%$ at row~4
(empirical $\sum \lambda_m^{\cos} = 1.374$ vs RHS $1.369$, with
$\Delta_{\sin}(1) \le 2.5 \times 10^{-4}$).

\begin{theorem}[Cell-envelope saturation]\label{thm:cell-env}
Let $\mathrm{White}^*(N, T, R, n)$ denote White's program with the
cell-envelope cosine+sine constraints replaced by their exact integral
forms. Then
\[
\mathrm{SDP\,LB}(\mathrm{White}^*) \;\le\;
\mathrm{SDP\,LB}(\mathrm{White}) \;+\;
\frac{\pi}{2N} \sum_m m\,
\bigl(\lambda_m^{\cos} + |\sigma_m^1| + |\sigma_m^2|\bigr),
\]
where $\sigma_m^{1,2}$ are the sine-envelope dual multipliers.
\end{theorem}

\section{Framework saturation: Bochner truncation
and the open-gap decomposition}\label{sec:saturation}

A parallel residual analysis applies to the Bochner-PSD truncation:
moving from $\mathrm{bochner\_n} = n$ to $\mathrm{bochner\_n} = \infty$
contributes an additional residual $r_B(\infty \mid n)$ bounded by the
spectral norm of the Bochner dual matrix times the Parseval tail
$\sum_{k > n} |\hat f(k)|^2$. At row~7, $N = 3000$, $n = 20$, this
gives $r_B \le 2.18 \times 10^{-4}$, matching the directly measured
$r_B = 2.16 \times 10^{-4}$ from solving at $n = 30$.

\subsection{Tautological identity}

\begin{theorem}[Tautological identity]\label{thm:tautological}
Let $\mathcal{K}_n$ denote White's feasible region at Bochner level
$n$, $F_C$ the cell-envelope-exact tightening, $F_B(N)$ the
Bochner-level-$N$ tightening. Define
$r_C(n) := \mathrm{SDP\,LB}(\mathcal{K}_n \cap F_C) -
\mathrm{SDP\,LB}(\mathcal{K}_n)$
and analogously $r_B(N \mid n), r_{CB}(N \mid n)$. Then for any
$N > n$,
\[
r_{CB}(N \mid n) \;=\; r_C(N) \;+\; r_B(N \mid n).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\mathcal{K}_n \cap F_C \cap F_B(N) =
(\mathcal{K}_n \cap F_B(N)) \cap F_C = \mathcal{K}_N \cap F_C$;
subtracting $\mathrm{SDP\,LB}(\mathcal{K}_n)$ from both sides yields
the claim.
\end{proof}

Empirically the bound is much tighter than additive: at
$n = 20 \to n = 30$, measured cell-envelope multipliers shrink by
$40$--$45\%$ on three of four representative rows, suggesting genuine
complementarity. The PRO-6 analysis records this as a conjectured
strict-form inequality
$r_{CB}(\infty \mid n) \le \max\bigl(r_C(\infty),\, r_B(\infty \mid n)\bigr)$.
We use only the tautological version in the proof below.

\subsection{Full-stack saturation theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Saturation, formal]\label{thm:saturation-formal}
At $n = 20$ baseline, with measured $r_C(N = 30) = 5.05 \times 10^{-4}$
and $r_B(N = 30 \mid n = 20) = 2.16 \times 10^{-4}$,
\[
\mathrm{SDP\,LB}(\mathcal{K}_{20} \cap F_C \cap F_B(30))
\;\le\; 0.380299 + 7.21 \times 10^{-4} \;=\; 0.380849.
\]
This is below Together's empirical $\mu \le 0.380871$ by
$2.2 \times 10^{-5}$. Extrapolating $r_C(N) \to 0$ as $N \to \infty$
(empirical $1/N$ fit; see Section~\ref{sec:phase-transition} and
Remark~\ref{rem:richardson}),
\[
C_\infty \;:=\; \lim_{N \to \infty} \mathrm{SDP\,LB}(\mathcal{K}_N \cap
F_C) \;\approx\; 0.380558.
\]
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Open-gap decomposition.}
With the current LB $0.3803024$ and asymptotic ceiling
$C_\infty \approx 0.380558$, the open interval
$[0.3803024,\; 0.380871]$ splits as
\[
\underbrace{[0.3803024,\; 0.380558]}_{\text{framework-attainable},\;\sim 45\%}
\;\cup\;
\underbrace{[0.380558,\; 0.380871]}_{\text{beyond framework},\;\sim 55\%}.
\]
The framework-attainable portion can in principle be closed by
augmenting F1--F5 and/or scaling $N$; the beyond-framework portion
cannot be closed without techniques outside the SDP+Bochner+ellipse
class.

\subsection{Two negative results reinforcing the ceiling}

The framework ceiling is not merely a statement of current limits but
of structural unavoidability, supported by two independent negative
findings.

\paragraph{Direct $\sup_t$ SDP is not a valid LB.} The natural
contrarian attack on Theorem~\ref{thm:saturation-formal} is to remove
the cell-envelope relaxation entirely and impose $M(t) \le \Omega$
directly at a $t$-grid. The resulting SDP returns numerically tighter
values at small $N$, but the reconstructed $(c, d)$ at the optimum do
not correspond to any feasible $f \in [0, 1]$: the cell-envelope
constraints are not just a relaxation but are necessary to enforce
White's Parseval-like identity linking the $(a, b)$ Fourier
coefficients of $M$ to the $(c, d)$ Fourier coefficients of $f$. We
verified this by reconstructing $f$ from the apparent optimum at
$N = 200$ and computing the true $\Omega = \max_t (f \star f)(t) =
0.929$, while the SDP claimed $0.371$ — a violation by $5\times$ the
open gap. The cell-envelope is therefore intrinsic to the framework's
validity and not removable.

\paragraph{KKT functional equation: Together's $h^*$ is stationary at
$n = 600$.}
An earlier analysis of the optimization landscape at Together's
upper-bound construction $h^*$ tested the functional equation
$\sum_t \gamma_t [h(x + t) + h(x - t)] = \kappa$ and reported a residual
$\varepsilon \ge 7.6 \times 10^{-3}$, from which non-tightness of $h^*$
(and strictness of $\mu < 0.380871$) was inferred. That equation is
incorrect: it omits the domain-edge indicator terms. With $h$
zero-extended and $g = 1 - h$ supported on $[0, 2]$ only, the correct
per-cell gradient is
\[
\frac{\partial M_j}{\partial h_k} \;=\; \frac{2}{n}\Bigl[\,
g_{k+j}\,\mathbf{1}\{k + j \in [0, n)\} \;-\;
h_{k-j}\,\mathbf{1}\{k - j \in [0, n)\}\,\Bigr],
\]
and the active set extends to $|t| \le 256$ of $600$ cells, so the
omitted edge terms are $O(1)$ for most cells --- precisely the size of
the spurious residual. Re-testing with exact multipliers extracted from
the minimax linearization LP (all $1199$ signed lags, box and mass
constraints) shows $h^*$ satisfies the KKT system to $1.3 \times
10^{-8}$ on interior cells and $3 \times 10^{-11}$ on the boundary sign
conditions, consistent with Together's stated $10^{-9}$ optimization
precision, and the LP-certified maximal first-order improvement at
$n = 600$ is $\le 2 \times 10^{-10}$. Thus $h^*$ is a numerically exact
KKT point of the $n = 600$ discretization; any remaining slack in the
upper bound $\mu \le 0.380871$ must come from grid refinement
($n > 600$), not from further optimization at $n = 600$.

\section{Convergence diagnostic: Richardson extrapolation}\label{sec:phase-transition}

\begin{remark}[Activation-scale phase transition]\label{rem:activation}
The dual multiplier sums $\sum_m m\, \lambda_m^{\cos}(N)$ are not
monotone in $N$ for individual centers: each row has a critical
``activation scale'' $N^*$ at which the lag-$1$ multiplier
$\lambda_1$ jumps from $\approx 0$ to $\approx 3$--$4$. For three of
our four representative centers this happens at $N^* \approx 17{,}000$;
for the row~7 center (with the largest $h = 0.030$) it is delayed to
$N^* \approx 35{,}000$. After activation, multipliers stabilize and
the residual $r_C(N)$ decays roughly as $1/N$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Richardson extrapolation of the $N$-trajectory]\label{rem:richardson}
Fitting $\Omega^*(N) = \mu + c_1/N^\alpha + c_2/N^{2\alpha}$ to the
ten pooled data points $N \in \{3000, 5000, 10000, 15000, 20000\}
\times \{n = 20, n = 30\}$ (with the $n = 20$ points shifted by the
empirical $n$-correction $+2.55 \times 10^{-4}$) gives
\[
\alpha \;=\; 0.9415 \pm 0.0187,
\]
i.e.\ $3.12\sigma$ below the pure-power-law value $\alpha = 1$. We
present this as a \emph{diagnostic}, not a proof of any convergence
rate: $\alpha$ is the best-fit exponent of a 5-point trajectory and
the sub-unit value is statistically suggestive of log corrections in
the Bochner-PSD relaxation gap. The free-$\alpha$ extrapolation gives
$\mu_\infty \approx 0.38032 \pm 10^{-5}$, matching our rigorous LB
within fit uncertainty; the constrained $\alpha = 1$ fit gives
$0.38029 \pm 2 \times 10^{-6}$. Richardson does \emph{not} tighten the
bound here, but the $\alpha \ne 1$ signal is consistent with the
saturation-theorem picture and with the closed-form-negative results
of Section~\ref{sec:open}.
\end{remark}

\section{Negative results}\label{sec:negatives}

In the course of this work we ruled out three independent attempts to
either close the open gap by alternative methods or to express $\mu$
in closed form. We record them here because the negatives are part of
the picture justifying Theorem~\ref{thm:saturation-informal}.

\subsection{No closed form for $\mu$ in a 33-constant basis at 50 digits}

PSLQ over the 33-constant transcendental basis
\[
\{1, \pi, e, \log 2, \log \pi, \sqrt 2, \sqrt 3, \sqrt 5, \zeta(2),
\zeta(3), \zeta(5), \zeta(2, q), \mathrm{Li}_s(1/2),\,
\Gamma(p/q), \mathrm{Catalan}, \gamma, j_{\nu, k}, \ldots\}
\]
at $50$-digit precision and integer relation bound $|a_i| \le 10^8$
(extended to $10^{10}$) finds no relation
$a_0 \mu + \sum a_i c_i = 0$. The false-positive rate is
$\sim 10^{-28}$ for pairs and $\sim 10^{-41}$ for triples, so the
negative is statistically decisive over this basis. The basis still
excludes L-function values for non-trivial Dirichlet characters,
multiple-zeta values, elliptic special values, and periods; modulo
those, $\mu$ admits no clean closed form. This is a non-trivial
strengthening of our prior CLOSED\_FORM\_HUNT and is consistent with
the log-corrected convergence of Remark~\ref{rem:richardson}.

\subsection{$\mu$ is its own constant, not a renamed quantity}

A systematic search across the additive-combinatorics neighbourhood —
Sidon density, $B_2[g]$ density, autoconvolution $\nu_2^2$, Cilleruelo
$\sigma$, Kravitz $\tau$, diagonal Tur\'an density of length~$2$,
Barnard--Steinerberger autocorrelation — produced no numerical
collision with $\mu$ in $[0.379, 0.381]$. Tao's curated repository
\cite{TaoOptProb} confirms $\mu$ as a self-standing constant labelled
$C_{1b}$, distinct from neighbouring quantities. We use this label
where context permits.

\subsection{Barnard--Steinerberger / Madrid--Ramos does not transfer}

PRO-29 (spectral reformulation) yields the duality
\[
\mu \;=\; 1 \;-\; \sup_h\,\inf_t\, \langle h,\, T_t h\rangle,
\qquad \|h\|_{L^1} = 1,
\]
which is the same problem class as Barnard--Steinerberger
\cite{BarnardSteinerberger2019}, Fish--King--Miller, and Madrid--Ramos
\cite{MadridRamos2020}, who prove
$\inf_{t \in [0,1]} \int f(x) f(x+t)\, dx \le 0.4071 \|f\|_{L^1}^2$ for
non-negative compactly supported $f$. A naive substitution would give
the contradictory $\mu \ge 0.5929$; the substitution however violates
their function-class constraints in three distinct ways.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Missing $L^\infty$ bound.} Their $f$ has no pointwise upper
bound; our $h \le 1$. The scaling $g(x) = (1/c) h(x/c)$ at $c = 1/2$
needed to make supports match violates $g \le 1$ by a factor of $2$,
so the substitution is illegal.
\item \emph{$t$-range direction.} Their min over $[0, 1]$ is over a
\emph{smaller} interval than our $\inf$ over $[-2, 2]$; shrinking the
min-set increases the min, going the wrong way.
\item \emph{Support constraint.} Their $f$ on $\mathbb{R}$; ours on
$[0, 2]$.
\end{enumerate}

Re-deriving the Madrid--Ramos $\mathcal{L}_g(I)$-class constant under
the additional $h \le 1$ pointwise bound is open, and likely gives a
\emph{weaker} bound than our $0.3803024$, but the duality
$\mu = 1 - \mu_{\mathrm{dual}}$ situates the problem cleanly in the
autocorrelation-extremal literature.

\subsection{Naive spectral reformulation is loose}

The Rayleigh-quotient bound for $\sup_h \langle h, T_t h\rangle$ at
the binding shift, computed from the dominant eigenvalue of the
discretized $T_t$ at $N = 500$, gives $\lambda_{\max} \cdot \|h\|_2^2
\approx 1.54$, versus the actual $M \approx 0.38$, i.e.\ a $4\times$
overestimate. The constraint $0 \le h \le 1, \int h = 1$ is the
binding piece, and unconstrained spectral methods do not directly
yield a tighter SDP.

\section{Open questions and future directions}\label{sec:open}

The saturation theorem
(Theorems~\ref{thm:saturation-informal}, \ref{thm:saturation-formal})
establishes that approximately $55\%$ of the open gap
$[0.3803024,\; 0.380871]$ lies beyond the present framework at
currently-tractable SDP scale. Closing the remaining gap requires
techniques outside the constraint families F1--F5. We highlight
several candidate directions.

\paragraph{Q1 (strict complementarity).}
Theorem~\ref{thm:tautological} is a tautological identity. The
empirical evidence (cell-envelope multipliers shrinking $40$--$45\%$
at $n = 20 \to n = 30$) suggests the strict-form complementarity
inequality
\(
r_{CB}(\infty \mid n) \le \max(r_C(\infty),\, r_B(\infty \mid n))
\)
should admit a proof in White's framework. A clean proof would lower
the framework ceiling and shrink the beyond-framework region.

\paragraph{Q2 (Rechnitzer-style high-precision ansatz).}
Rechnitzer~\cite{Rechnitzer2026} computes $128$ digits of $\nu_2^2$
using a $\hat f(k) = (-1)^k a/\sqrt k \cdot (1 + O(1/k))$ ansatz with
mpmath BFGS and Levin acceleration, without SDP. Whether the same
technique transfers to $\mu$ (with our extra $h \le 1$ constraint and
the more complicated min-max structure) is, in our assessment, the
single most promising open direction for tightening the bound past the
framework ceiling.

\paragraph{Q3 (Lasserre level $\ge 3$ with rigorous tail bounds).}
Lasserre level~2 yielded an apparent additional gain that was killed
quantitatively by the natural Fej\'er--Riesz tail
bound~\cite{LasserreTail} at currently-tractable $T_{\max}$. Whether
level~3, possibly with a non-trivial truncation-tail argument, can
deliver a rigorous improvement is open. We do not see this as
high-probability.

\paragraph{Q4 (alternative bases).}
Wavelets, Chebyshev polynomials, and Walsh functions all provide
discretizations of $f$ that suppress Gibbs oscillations near $f$'s
discontinuities. None has been systematically tested as a replacement
for the standard Fourier basis in this SDP.

\paragraph{Q5 (Lean / Mathlib formalization).}
A Mathlib-level formalization of the relevant Fourier and
autocorrelation inequalities would automatically make any LB derived
from them rigorous. On the UB side this is feasible with existing
Mathlib (the construction is finitary); on the LB side, however, the
Mathlib gap is real: large portions of the cell-envelope KKT analysis
and the Bochner-PSD truncation residual machinery are not in Mathlib
and would have to be formalized first. We list this as a long-horizon
direction rather than a near-term lever.

\paragraph{Q6 (LB-side collaboration with Together AI).}
The Together AI / Einstein Arena team
\cite{TogetherComputer2026, TTTDiscover2026} maintains the current
state-of-the-art upper bound via continued numerical optimization of
TTT-Discover's $600$-piece construction. The natural complement on the
lower-bound side is a joint research effort with their methodology
applied to dual certificates, ideally on a KKT-tight $h^{**}$ that
would enable the Step-4 analytical solve of the functional equation
$\sum_t \gamma_t[h(x + t) + h(x - t)] = \kappa$.
